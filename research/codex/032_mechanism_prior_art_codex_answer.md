**The broad mechanism claim is already prior art.** Constraint handling can alter adaptation dynamics, and numerical constraint improvements already feed parameter adaptation. I56’s remaining distinction is its particular controlled intervention; priority for that exact design remains unestablished.

Closest works, with verified DOIs:

- **Arnold (2012), “On the Behaviour of the (1,λ)-σSA-ES…”** derives constrained self-adaptation dynamics and predicts convergence to nonstationary points for small objective/constraint angles. **Arnold (2013), “Resampling versus Repair…”**, explains qualitatively different cumulative step-size adaptation (CSA) behavior under different CHTs. These directly precede the general interaction hypothesis. DOIs: [10.1007/978-3-642-32937-1_9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-32937-1_9); [10.1162/EVCO_a_00088](https://doi.org/10.1162/EVCO_a_00088).

- **Hellwig & Arnold (2016), “Comparison of Constraint-Handling Mechanisms…”**, *Evolutionary Computation* 24(1), compares reflection and truncation with earlier approaches on a constrained linear problem. One-generation distributions and steady-state analysis explain differences under CSA. Thus, **CHT choice affecting adaptation is explicitly established**, although through repaired steps rather than GAdapt’s merit-value dispersion. DOI: [10.1162/EVCO_a_00139](https://doi.org/10.1162/EVCO_a_00139).

- **Arnold & Hansen (2012), “A (1+1)-CMA-ES for Constrained Optimisation”**, accumulates constraint-violating steps to estimate boundary normals and reduce mutation variance in those directions. It uses binary feasibility information rather than violation magnitudes—an existing alternative information route into adaptation, without I56’s numerical-signal intervention. DOI: [10.1145/2330163.2330207](https://doi.org/10.1145/2330163.2330207).

- **Atamna, Auger & Hansen (2016; 2020)** combine adaptive augmented-Lagrangian constraint handling with adaptive ES/CMA-ES. Their later analysis explicitly identifies loss of invariance under general increasing objective transformations, formalizes retained affine/scaling invariances, and connects invariance with conditional linear convergence. **Important distinction:** transforming the objective before constructing an augmented Lagrangian can change penalized ordering; this is different from transforming an already constructed merit while preserving its ordering. DOIs: [10.1007/978-3-319-45823-6_17](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-45823-6_17); [10.1016/j.tcs.2018.10.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2018.10.006).

- **Spettel & Beyer (2019; 2020)** derive and numerically check mutation-strength and CSA dynamics under projection repair on conically constrained problems. These strengthen the theoretical precedent for constraint-induced adaptation dynamics, without addressing penalized fitness-diversity signals. DOIs: [10.1016/j.tcs.2018.10.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2018.10.036); [10.1162/evco_a_00261](https://doi.org/10.1162/evco_a_00261).

- **Zamuda (2017), CAL-SHADE**, is especially close: §III explicitly gives constraint-violation improvements precedence when computing differences for success-history adaptation. Thus, **constraint-dependent numerical feedback into parameter adaptation predates I56**. The checked paper does not isolate this through a common-signal or ordering-preserving intervention. DOI: [10.1109/CEC.2017.7969601](https://doi.org/10.1109/CEC.2017.7969601).

- **Sakamoto & Akimoto (2022), ARCH**, modifies CMA-ES through candidate rankings and proves/empirically checks transformation invariances. This supplies an explicit ordering-based precedent, but not a matched ordering-versus-value ablation. Fitness-value-driven GA mutation/diversity control itself also predates GAdapt: **Srinivas & Patnaik (1994)**. DOIs: [10.1162/evco_a_00310](https://doi.org/10.1162/evco_a_00310); [10.1109/21.286385](https://doi.org/10.1109/21.286385).

**Precise remaining difference:** I found no exact predecessor combining GAdapt’s complete mutation-policy ablation × CHT × discretization resolution with both SP/AD-N versus SP/AD-C and SP versus SP-T. SP-T preserves comparator ordering **for any given candidate set**; subsequent populations can diverge through adaptation. AD-C changes both numerical signals while retaining the CHT’s selection rule. Neither identifies a mediated fraction, and the common FR scalar remains value-sensitive. GAdapt’s merit spread also measures neither genotypic diversity nor feasibility independently.
