## Abstract

Claims about optimization performance depend on the true sequence of objective evaluations, consistency of the optimizer's reported best value with visited points, deterministic replay and feasibility under domain constraints. These properties cannot always be inferred from the apparent iteration count or the advertised interface. We synthesize a preregistered sequence of black-box evaluation-integrity studies across Python optimization libraries and an AC power-dispatch simulator, preserving corrections, negative controls and incomplete observations. The initial study classified at least one integrity-related finding in 4/9 eligible libraries (95% Clopper–Pearson interval 0.14–0.79); all individual findings had known precedents. In an independently specified cohort, an initial headline flag rate of 11/12 became 4/12 once a nonspecific corner-point detector was excluded; two previously unreported documentation contradictions were identified. A preregistered cross-domain comparison found agreement in library-level non-C1 status in 19/21 cases (95% interval 0.70–0.99), while an AC-dispatch problem exposed a reported-versus-visited-best discrepancy in SciPy's polishing path. A subsequent hash-seed-controlled archive experiment confirmed its manipulation in 620/620 evaluable cells, but its strict bitwise-replication hypothesis failed due to a separate numerical-thread configuration. Additional later software-only numerical AC checks reproduced one development case's *reactive infeasibility* and distinguished objective calls from OPF/PF numerical solver calls. Together, these studies motivate independent trace-based auditing with explicit negative controls, model and environment provenance, and resource-type accounting. The findings **do not** establish a universally accurate detector, independent field measurements, or new optimization theory, and the manuscript's original contribution remains subject to independent prior-art and supervisor review.

**Keywords:** evolutionary algorithms, experimental integrity, black-box optimization, budget accounting, reproducibility, AC power flow, false-positive detection.

## 1. Introduction and narrow research question

An optimization algorithm may legitimately use initialization probes, repeat previously visited candidates, call a secondary numerical solver inside one objective evaluation, or report a result whose evaluation history is not straightforward. These possibilities complicate cross-framework and engineering comparisons. We ask whether **the recorded and independently inspected objective-call sequence** can expose discrepancies in the reported solution, budget and stochastic replay, and whether conclusions found on synthetic objective functions transfer to an AC electrical network **simulation**.

This work does **not** claim that a corner-point evaluation is necessarily a hidden probe, that every documented behavior is a bug, or that a numerically converged power-flow operating point is feasible. Its scientific evaluation demands both defect injection and benign controls.

## 2. Study design, evidence provenance, and definitions

The reported program consists of I49 (initial broad synthetic instrumentation audit), I50 (new roster and a refined reproducibility manipulation), I52 (AC-dispatch transfer) and I55 (trace-archive verification). The historical preregistrations and their own amendments govern each study separately; **do not merge their samples into an unqualified meta-experiment**. The initial cohort includes 10 tested libraries but only **nine eligible** for the specified prevalence denominator; the second included 12, leading to 21 eligible library entries over two study rosters. Study-level eligibility and environment configurations are fully specified in the frozen reports.

The tools classify, among others, test invocations/corners (C1), duplicate candidates (C2), out-of-range evaluations (C3), nominal budget violations (C4), post-return cost calls (C5), incorrect reported best (R2/R3) and stochastic replay inconsistencies (D3/D3x). A flag does not automatically mean **an undocumented bug**. I50 distinguishes a legitimate candidate at the bound from a hidden non-search probe; I49 separates explicit documentation contradiction from documented API policy. Validity requires the actual objective trace and original library contract, not just a detector's label.

The record includes separate injected mutants and clean controls, source SHA fingerprints, pinned environments, original output records and manual causal follow-up. The datasets are **synthetic mathematical functions** and **PYPOWER-solved AC simulator networks**; no field-measured electric grid data are claimed.

## 3. Observed results, including disconfirmations

### 3.1 I49: initial synthetic audit

Frozen [I49 report](legacy/flat/ITERATION49_REPORT_SR.md) records 300 cells across library/problem/seed combinations and a 53-second primary pass. Five injected failure classes were detected while the clean control was not flagged. The preregistered primary prevalence outcome was **4/9** libraries, 95% Clopper–Pearson interval **[0.14,0.79]**. The four named positive cases were DEAP out-of-bounds (documented design), PyGAD post-return population reevaluation (documented), pycma over-advertised iterationwise `maxfevals`, and Nevergrad seeded reproducibility (previously filed). **None of the four individual bugs was first found in this work.** Restricting to cases actually contradicting documentation yields 2/9 (a labelled post hoc analysis). This denominator must not be presented as a universal defect prevalence.

### 3.2 I50: independent cohort and C1 specificity failure

Frozen [I50 report](legacy/flat/ITERATION50_REPORT_SR.md) records a separate one-time preregistered run. Its primary headline was **11/12** libraries with at least one flag, but **C1 was nonspecific**: legitimate search visits to a domain corner, optimizer initialization and plateau optima were frequently counted as suspicious. Excluding C1 reduces prevalence to **4/12**, a post hoc sensitivity that would render the original prevalence judgment undecided. Predeclared documentation-contradiction flags were **2/12**: GFO sign inversion for `best_score` and SMAC's hash-seed-dependent initial search order. The report contains downstream issue references and root-cause checks; these are software findings, not independent demonstrations that an optimizer is universally unreliable.

### 3.3 I52: transfer to actual AC-dispatch simulations

Frozen [I52 report](legacy/flat/ITERATION52_REPORT_SR.md) records one technical first-run failure *before any objective evaluations*, caused by PYPOWER/NumPy environment incompatibility, and its openly documented environment amendment. The preregistered primary comparison of non-C1 library status between synthetic and AC-dispatch evaluation agreed in **19/21**, 95% interval **[0.70,0.99]**, above the prespecified threshold of 17.

However, transfer was **not perfect**: Nevergrad's D3/D3x did not reproduce under the evaluable AC cells; SciPy DE produced a distinct reported-best discrepancy during unsuccessful polishing, visible in **5/15** dispatch trials. One case consumed 15,444 polishing evaluations before returning a worse value than one it visited. The report also identified repeat calls and some resource costs that depended strongly on the problem family. The case14/case39/case118 runs were **numerical power-grid simulation**, not independent network field trials.

### 3.4 I55: corrected hash-seed manipulation and a strict negative

Frozen [I55 report](legacy/flat/ITERATION55_REPORT_SR.md) confirmed the seed manipulation in **620/620 valid** process comparisons (10 Nevergrad cells invalid due to environment failure). SMAC's interprocess D3x occurred in **30/30** tested cells with different hash seeds, with no same-hash-seed D3x; Nevergrad had residual issues in **5/20** evaluable cells. A strict preregistered bitwise archive-replay hypothesis was **not supported** due to an emukit cell affected by `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS` configuration, identified post hoc. Consequently, claiming perfect reproducibility from pinned algorithm seeds and Python versions alone would be false.

### 3.5 Subsequent independent AC numerical verification (engineering supplement, not a new performance result)

The separate [Newton + reconstructed dispatch CI report](I65_INDEPENDENT_DISPATCH_RESULT_2026-11-02.md) documents **47/47** analytic/negative tests and one fixed pre-used development case, in which independent NumPy AC software reproduces original PYPOWER generator and branch outputs to numerical precision. Yet the *same* fixed midpoint is **reactively infeasible** (NumPy penalty `0.5991137833375704`, PYPOWER penalty `0.5991137833375748`). Neither independent software agreement nor solver convergence establishes legal reactive dispatch. In a separate [API-ledger trace](I65_ORPD_SOLVER_ENTRY_AUDIT_RESULT_2026-11-02.md), **one** I56 candidate evaluator was accompanied by **2 OPF + 2 PF** API entries including setup and witness, illustrating why objective-attempt counts and solver-API resource counts must remain distinct. The PYPOWER internal work was **not instrumented completely**.

## 4. Interpretation and claims that the data do not support

The observations support that *selected instrumentation checks can disclose material mismatches between advertised and observed software behavior*, and that non-C1 library-level findings have partial cross-domain consistency in a preregistered transfer study. They do **not** show that every flagged event is an error: the I50 C1 counterexample falsifies that assumption. Nor does 19/21 prove that the tool is statistically calibrated over all networks or optimization libraries: libraries are not uniformly randomly sampled and the domains overlap. The known positive findings are generally documented or already filed, with a few newly investigated instances; all such attribution should be separately verified against the original issues.

The obvious follow-up — blind cross-domain false-positive/false-negative validation with legitimate corner samples and nested PF calls — is **not part of the completed frozen historical studies**. No new candidate/result from such a follow-up is reported here. Such a study must be reviewed and preregistered if it becomes scientifically necessary.

## 5. Practical recommendation

Optimization benchmarking reports should distinguish (i) **wrapper objective-attempts** (including initial probes and failures), (ii) **actual numerical solver API entrypoints** in setup/search/validation, (iii) candidate proposals and duplicate cache hits, (iv) independent **returned-versus-best-visited** result checks, (v) archived deterministic environment including hash seeds and BLAS threading, and (vi) final numerical convergence **versus** externally defined operational-feasibility constraints. A clean outcome in one layer does not certify the others. These are practical engineering recommendations, **not a novel theoretical runtime bound**.
